// OMICS MODULES

Three Omics, One Pipeline

Each module handles its own data type with free, curated tools — designed for users with minimal bioinformatics experience.

🧬
Metagenomics
WHO is there?
  • 01Raw read quality check & trimming FastQC + Trimmomatic
  • 02Host DNA removal (human reads) Bowtie2
  • 03Taxonomic classification at species level Kraken2 / MetaPhlAn4
  • 04Functional gene annotation HUMAnN3
  • 05Diversity analysis (alpha & beta) QIIME2
  • 06African reference DB comparison AWI-Gen / MicrobiomeDB
Free Tools
FastQC Kraken2 MetaPhlAn4 HUMAnN3 QIIME2 Bracken
🔬
Metatranscriptomics
WHAT are they doing?
  • 01rRNA depletion & QC SortMeRNA
  • 02Read alignment to metagenome assembly STAR / HISAT2
  • 03Active gene expression quantification HTSeq / featureCounts
  • 04Differential expression analysis DESeq2
  • 05Pathway & metabolic activity HUMAnN3
  • 06Cross-compare to metagenomic potential R integration
Free Tools
SortMeRNA STAR DESeq2 HTSeq Salmon edgeR
⚗️
Metabolomics
WHAT do they produce?
  • 01Raw spectral data processing (LC-MS/MS) MZmine3
  • 02Peak detection, alignment, normalisation XCMS
  • 03Metabolite annotation & ID GNPS / SIRIUS
  • 04Statistical analysis & PCA MetaboAnalyst
  • 05Pathway enrichment mapping KEGG / BioCyc
  • 06Microbiome-metabolite correlation MaAsLin2
Free Tools

// MULTI-OMICS INTEGRATION

Connecting the Three Layers

Integration reveals what no single omics layer can — the active metabolic conversation between microbes, their gene expression, and measurable chemical outputs.

🔗 AfriOmics Integration Hub
MOFA+ · DIABLO · mixOmics · SparCC
🔗
Species ↔ Metabolite
Correlate which microbial taxa drive specific metabolite production — e.g. butyrate-producing Firmicutes in African gut samples.
⚡
Gene Expression ↔ Function
Map actively transcribed genes to metabolic pathways — distinguishing who CAN do something vs. who IS doing it now.
🕸️
Cross-Site Network
Model interactions between body sites — how gut dysbiosis may signal to the oral, vaginal, or nasal microbiome in African populations.
🤖
ML-Based Prediction
Train classifiers on integrated multi-omics features to predict disease risk, disease state, or treatment response.

🌍 African Reference Databases
H3Africa / AWI-Gen Cohort
Pan-African genomic and microbiome dataset spanning West, East, Southern & Central Africa. Integrated as default comparative context in AfriOmics.
MicrobiomeDB African Subset
Curated microbiome studies from African populations — allows your sample results to be instantly contextualized against known African gut profiles.
GNPS African Metabolome
Spectral libraries enriched with metabolites relevant to African dietary patterns, including fermented foods, traditional plants, and regional nutrition sources.
Continent-Aware Diversity Indices
Alpha and beta diversity benchmarks derived specifically from African populations — avoiding bias from predominantly European / North American reference microbiomes.
Traditional Diet & Lifestyle Metadata
Optional metadata fields for diet type (e.g. fermented cassava, millet porridge), urbanisation level, water source — key confounders in African microbiome research.
// DISEASE MODELLING

Gut–Disease Interaction Models

AfriOmics uses integrated multi-omics signatures to model how the gut (and other body site) microbiome interacts with endemic African infectious diseases.

🦟
Malaria (P. falciparum)
Model gut microbiome composition shifts during acute vs. chronic malaria; identify metabolites that mediate parasite-host interactions.
RELEVANCE: HIGH — Sub-Saharan Africa
🦠
HIV / AIDS
Track microbial translocation, gut permeability biomarkers, and microbiome-driven immune activation across ART treatment stages.
RELEVANCE: CRITICAL — >70% global burden in Africa
🫁
Tuberculosis (TB)
Identify gut-lung axis signatures in TB patients; correlate metabolomic profiles with treatment response and drug resistance.
RELEVANCE: HIGH — 30% global TB in Africa
🩺
Schistosomiasis
Characterise helminth-microbiome crosstalk and immunomodulatory metabolites — a neglected tropical disease with major African burden.
RELEVANCE: HIGH — NTD endemic
💧
Cholera / Diarrhoeal Disease
Model protective vs. susceptible microbiome configurations; link SCFA production to colonisation resistance in Vibrio cholerae.
RELEVANCE: HIGH — WASH-linked
🩹
Sepsis / Systemic Infection
Integrate gut dysbiosis markers, blood metabolomics, and microbial translocation to predict sepsis severity in African ICU settings.
RELEVANCE: MODERATE-HIGH
// ACCESSIBILITY BY DESIGN

Built for Everyone

Designed so that a researcher with a laptop and a dataset can run a full multi-omics pipeline without writing a single line of code — if they choose.

🖱️
No-Code GUI Mode
Upload your files, click through a guided wizard, and receive a full interactive report. Powered by a Shiny web app deployable locally or via cloud.
📓
Jupyter Notebook Workflows
Step-by-step notebooks for each module with explanatory text, interactive plots, and modifiable parameters for those learning bioinformatics.
🐚
CLI Pipeline (Snakemake)
Advanced users get a fully reproducible Snakemake workflow for HPC/cluster execution — runs on local machines, university HPC, or Google Colab.
☁️
Cloud-Ready (Free Tiers)
Pre-configured for Google Colab, Galaxy (usegalaxy.eu), and GitHub Codespaces — no local software installation needed for basic runs.
📚
Contextual Explanations
Every result comes with plain-language interpretation, African microbiome context, and links to relevant literature. Not just numbers — meaning.
🌐
Multilingual Support (Planned)
Interface and documentation planned for French, Swahili, Hausa and Portuguese — to reach researchers across all African linguistic regions.
